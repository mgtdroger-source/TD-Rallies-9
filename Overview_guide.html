TD Rallies — From Route to Results

Introduction

Time and Distance Rallies is an integrated rally planning and results system. It supports rallies of up to seven days, with up to four speed groups, optionally two routes on a rally day. Rally Controls can be physical or virtual Marshals, Open Controls and Self Timed Controls (STC's) at which entrants record their elapsed rally time when passing a defined location.

Competitor entries can be gathered using Google forms saving a lot of typing. On a rally day entrant timings are shared either via Google forms or mobile apps enabling results to be processed and presented with minimal effort. All the necessary tools are integrated into TD Rallies.

Only basic computer skills are needed. TD Rallies guides you through the main tasks, with detailed Help available when required.

Part 1 · The organiser's journey

From route to results

The following five stages show how TD Rallies supports the work of organising a rally. The second part of this guide introduces what you will actually see on each page.

1. Every rally begins with a route

As an organiser you probably already have a good idea of where you want the rally to go. TD Rallies gives you a choice of how to turn that idea into a route schedule.

You can enter route instructions and distances directly in Schedule, or build the route on the Mapping page, you can also gather waypoints, control positions and distances in the field with the GPS-based Rally Field Tool. Field data can be brought into Mapping for refinement before the route is imported into Schedule.

There is no requirement to begin with GPS mapping; use the method that best suits you.

2. Getting your rally underway

While developing the route, you can get the entry process underway. In Admin, give the rally a title, establish a workspace for saving your work, and set the rally days, dates, starting times and speed groups.

Entries can be entered manually. Alternatively, the one-time Google connection allows you to share an entry form and download completed entries directly into the Admin page, saving a lot of typing. Before the rally, entrants can be sorted into speed groups and the starting order and start times established.

3. Developing the rally schedule

With a route in place, Schedule brings together distances, instructions, speeds, Added Time and controls. As you choose the speed for each section and speed group, ETA times are calculated automatically.

Adding an STC, Marshal, Open Control or Start STC also prepares the corresponding ordered cards in Controls. The optional Timeline gives a visual view of how speed groups and entrants progress along the route, helping you consider spacing, start delays and breaks.

The result is a schedule ready for checking and printing.

4. Getting ready for rally day

Decide how control times will be collected. Physical marshals can return their times using Google forms, while entrants can submit STC times using forms or the Rally Recorder app.

The Recorder link if shared in Button mode, the entrant records STC times by pressing a button at a location. GPS location based timings are also recorded in the background for later audit review. Both STC and Virtual marshal times are recorded in this way. In GPS mode, there are no buttons to be pushed, times are gathered automatically based on GPS location. Tracking is another optional choice if the organiser wants to monitor entrant progress during the event.

When preparations are final, upload the day's Controls data and share the appropriate daily forms and Rally Recorder links.

5. Running the rally and producing results

During the rally, entrants and marshals record or submit their times using the selected methods. The organiser can optionally follow entrants through Tracking, either on the desktop or using the organiser's Tracking PWA on a mobile device.

After the rally, review the returned times and download them into Controls. Expected and actual times are compared, penalties are calculated, and the organiser or Clerk of the Course can audit results, apply additional penalties or exclude a control where appropriate. Results can then be printed or exported.

Part 2 · Getting comfortable with TD Rallies

The basics — what you will see and use

These introductions give you enough familiarity to explore each page. The individual guides explain the detailed procedures.

Admin — the rally and its entrants

Begin by entering the rally title. Set the days and dates (up to seven), start times, starting interval and speed groups (up to four). Each rally day can use one or two routes. Use Tools to establish the Rally Workspace, where your project files are saved.

  • Entries opens the Google setup and entry-management card. Complete the Google connection once, then upload entry details and share the entry form if you want online entries.
  • Use Download entry data to populate the entrant table when entries arrive, or enter details manually.
  • Sort arranges the entrants into starting order by speed group. Rally numbers and their start-time positions remain fixed as entrants are sorted. Start Delays can be included for a day where required.
  • Save and Load work with the entire rally project, not just Admin. Saving before major changes or uploads is good practice.

Schedule — instructions, speeds and times

Select the required day and route. Use ADD ROW to add instructions, control points or Added Time, or choose Import Mapped Route when a route has been prepared in Mapping.

  • ADD ROW opens a menu for adding instructions, STCs, Marshals, Open Controls, Start STCs or Added Time. Controls included in Schedule automatically create the corresponding cards in Controls.
  • Distances and speeds: New values have an orange ring, which clears once edited or confirmed. A red error border flags a distance lower than the preceding row.
  • Colours and printing: Blue text indicates calculated or non-editable information. Black text is normally editable and printed. Red text is excluded from normal print. Distance and time tick boxes turn selected values red; unchanged speeds are also suppressed automatically.
  • Speed Groups: Select a group in the button panel to display its speed and time card. Each group can have its own speeds and timings. If a row's time is excluded from print, the corresponding speed-group times are excluded too.
  • Added Time: Allowances for traffic lights or stops can be set independently for each speed group. The row includes a reason selector.
  • Turn column: Choose turn abbreviations in the final column. Click the Turn heading to exclude that column from print; its text becomes red.
  • Timeline shows the movement and spacing of speed groups along the route. Before entrant details are finalised, use Cars/SG for planning. Start Delays can be explored in Timeline and applied to that rally day using Include Start Delays in Admin.

A Start STC establishes a fresh timing reference: Schedule displays 00:00:00 at that point. See the Schedule and Controls guides for the timing details.

Mapping — an optional route-building tool

Mapping lets you develop the route on a map, adding waypoints and controls in driving order. You can inspect locations using map and street-view tools, add route-shaping breadcrumbs, and import surveyed positions from the Rally Field Tool.

  • Save each map to its day and route folder. When the route is ready, import the saved map into the matching Schedule.
  • Changes to mapped control positions are made in Mapping and re-imported into Schedule.
  • For STCs and Virtual Marshals, trip lines are recommended for best timing accuracy, but they are optional: the Rally Recorder can use GPS radius detection when a trip line is not defined.
  • Mapping includes distance calibration, allowing calculated route distances to be adjusted against a preferred measured reference.
  • The Rally Field Tool also supports checking a prepared route against distances and points measured during a drive.

Controls — times and results

Controls displays the entrants and the STC, Marshal, Open Control and Start STC cards created by Schedule. ETA is the calculated expected arrival time; ATA is the recorded actual time. You can enter times directly or download submitted times from Google.

  • Select an entrant's rally number to review or enter that entrant's control times together.
  • Controls data prepares the day's online forms and Rally Recorder links. The organiser chooses the timing mode and whether Virtual Marshals or Tracking are used.
  • After timing returns have been checked, select Download controls data. The results update automatically.
  • Use the Audit facility to identify unusual errors. Controls may be excluded from scoring where appropriate; Clerk of the Course penalties can also be added.
  • Print lets you select a report and, where applicable, how it is sorted. Results can also be exported to a spreadsheet.
Important: uploading Controls data again can clear timing returns for that day in Google. Review and download any results you need to keep, and save the rally project before rebuilding the day's data.

Tracking — optional monitoring

When enabled in Controls, Rally Recorder can send periodic entrant GPS positions during the rally. The Tracking page shows the last reported positions on the event map, while Event Replay lets the organiser review previously recorded movements.

The organiser can also use the Tracking PWA to view progress on a mobile device. This PWA is for monitoring entrants; it is not a separate recording app that entrants must install.

A displayed position is the last one received, not proof of an entrant's exact present location.

Part 3 · Explore when useful

More possibilities, at your pace

Two-route rallies

Run two different routes on the same rally day, with entrants allocated to either route. Schedule and Mapping keep the routes separate while Controls calculates results for each entrant's assignment. Both routes must have matching control arrangements.

Rally Recorder choices

Use the PWA on a compatible phone, or the installed Android Recorder. Button and GPS modes, Virtual Marshals and optional Tracking allow organisers to choose how much timing work is automated.

Field verification

Use the Rally Field Tool to survey locations, check the route in the field and compare measured distances with mapped distances before finalising the Schedule.

Dashboard at a glance

The Dashboard brings together summaries of Admin, Schedule and Controls, including entries, rally-day distances and timings, and control-data activity.

These facilities are available when they help your event. A straightforward single-route rally with manually entered results is also a perfectly valid way to use TD Rallies.

Where to go next

Detailed guides when you need them

The individual Admin, Schedule, Controls, Mapping and Tracking guides provide the step-by-step instructions behind the features introduced here. The Google Setting Up Guide, available through the Admin Entries card, covers the initial Google connection.

Begin with Admin to create the rally and workspace, then use the other pages as your plans develop. There is no need to learn the whole system before starting.
